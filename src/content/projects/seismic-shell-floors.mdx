## Question

A lighter floor cuts the seismic mass, so the beams, columns and foundations can get smaller. The same floor has less in-plane stiffness, and past some point it stops acting as a rigid diaphragm. Topping and ties then put carbon back. Where is that trade-off, and which floor geometry reaches it?

## Findings in brief

There is no design result yet. The question is fixed. The floor geometries have not been optimised, and this page states no carbon comparison.

<More label="See the method and the checks">

## Approach

The study is a shell floor inside a whole reinforced-concrete frame. The floor has to carry its own gravity and the in-plane shear of the earthquake. The pieces are a mechanics model of the floor, a check that it still works as a diaphragm, and a search over rise, thickness and ribs. ShellBench supplies the frame, the carbon count and the diaphragm test. It is imported, not edited.

## Validation

The validation ladder is fixed before the design runs. The first two mechanics checks have passed. The remaining rungs, and the search over geometries, have not been run.

</More>

## What this does not show

- No optimum shape, and no saving in whole-building carbon. Those are the result the study is for, and they do not exist yet.
- The code is not released. The checks stay in the working repository until the study is finished.
- A passed mechanics check is not a statement about a building.
