## Question

A lighter floor cuts the seismic mass, so the beams, columns and foundations can get smaller. The same floor has less in-plane stiffness, and past some point it stops acting as a rigid diaphragm. Topping and ties then put carbon back. Where is that trade-off, and which floor geometry reaches it?

## Findings in brief

There is no carbon optimum, and no whole-building result. The record is one bay: its in-plane stiffness, a diaphragm ratio on the anchor spans, and gravity on that bay. At equal volume, a rise cuts the in-plane stiffness, and one rib cross does not give it back.

## Approach

The bay is 3 m square. The surface is a sine vault, and the rises are 0.30 m and 0.60 m. The concrete is C30/37, E = 33 GPa and ν = 0.2. The mesh is a grid of CalculiX S8R shells. The stiffness is the plan membrane stiffness under three affine strains. Linear static, with no geometric stiffness.

Equal volume means the faceted shell matches a flat plate 60 mm thick. The cross is one element wide, 3/21 m, on a 50 mm shell. The rib thickness is whatever that volume requires: 142.2 mm at rise 0.30 m and 102.5 mm at rise 0.60 m. The surface, the rises, the cross and the 50 mm shell are still to be confirmed.

At rise 0.60 m the cross is 0.95637 of the smooth shell in extension and 0.96369 in shear. At rise 0.30 m those ratios are 0.92757 and 0.91777.

The diaphragm ratio is alpha = K_d / K_f. K_d is the simply-supported Timoshenko stiffness of one bay, with the cell Et and Gt and k = 5/6. K_f is the ground-storey stiffness of one transverse frame, 1.174970e8 N/m, at a height of 4.55 m. The floor model is that beam on a spring at each frame line. The anchor spans are 6.0, 6.6, 6.6, 6.6, 6.6 and 6.0 m, and the depth is 21.0 m. A 3 m cell enters only through Et and Gt. The author confirms the storey and that step. Two frames, from the definition of K_d, give alpha* = 20. The beam element returns 19.9956.

A joint here is a centreline strip of elements, one element wide, with the modulus multiplied by a factor. Factor 1 is the continuous floor. The strip width is the element width, so the number moves with the mesh. The author confirms the strip.

An earlier mesh, 16×16, printed the smooth rise of 0.60 m as 0.44562 against its own 54.9 mm thickness (`partE_validation/out/vault_bay.txt`). Dividing by the area ratio 1.0930 gives 0.40770 against the 60 mm plate, beside 0.40763 on the 21×21 mesh.

## Validation

Rungs 1 and 2 have a pass mark. The curved stiffness rows are the record, and they have no separate pass mark.

- Rung 1, a flat cell against exact E t and G t: worst error 1.62e-06%, limit 1%.
- Rung 2, a deep beam against Timoshenko: worst error 0.21%, limit 2%. Shear carries 3.5% to 36.5% of the deflection.
- Rise zero on the smooth bay matches the flat cell within 0.1%: Et 1.684e-06% and Gt 1.155e-12%.
- Rise zero on the rib mesh is 1.00000 and 1.00000 against E T and G T.
- Rung 4, a 3 m deep beam against Timoshenko using Et and Gt from the same mesh: flat −2.93%, inside 5%. Smooth rise 0.60 m +15.80%, outside 5%, on the 12×12 mesh. On the recorded 21×21 mesh the smooth shell is +15.33% and the cross is +31.53%. Both are outside 5%. The full floor is not in this check.
- An X-strut grid, calibrated on Et and Gt and then checked on the flat deep beam: +9.83% at a 1 m panel and +12.74% at 0.5 m. Both outside 5%. That 5% is the brief's limit, not a code clause.
- Two-frame alpha*: 19.9956 against 20 from the definition. The beam element matches the closed deflection to −0.003%.

## What this does not show

- No optimum shape, and no saving in whole-building carbon. The search over geometries has not been run.
- One cross, diagonals at two rises and two stress-line spacings are not the ribbed floor. Hypothesis H1, that a flat plate is an upper bound for a curved ribbed floor of equal volume, stays open.
- Hypothesis H2 is not supported on this reduced model. alpha* is 54.651 at L/D 0.3 and 495.420 at L/D 2.
- The rise 0.60 m shell is 15.80% off the Timoshenko beam that uses its own Et and Gt. On the recorded 21×21 mesh that shell is 15.33% off, and the cross is 31.53% off. The homogenised stiffness is not a 5% substitute for that vault.
- Gravity factors of several thousand are for self-weight on this bay. No knockdown is applied. They are not a design check of a building.
- The code is not released. A passed mechanics check is not a statement about a building.
