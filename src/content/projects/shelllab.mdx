## Question

What shape carries a shell's own weight in pure compression, and what does that shape ask of its supports? ShellForm answers this for a fixed set of designs. ShellLab makes the same solver something a designer can push around live.

## Approach

- **Force density method** (Schek, 1974). With the force densities fixed, equilibrium at the free nodes is linear, so each coordinate comes from one banded Cholesky solve.
- **Rectangular plans** from 6 to 40 m a side, supports from presets or placed by clicking nodes, an edge tie that stops free edges drawing in, and lateral load in x and y.
- **Readouts**: bar forces, horizontal thrust at every support, vertical reactions, the equilibrium residual, concrete volume and A1–A3 carbon (C40, 340 kgCO₂e/m³, from ShellBench) with a ±25 % band.
- Export as JSON or OBJ, and a link that holds the whole design. No build step, no CDN, no server.

## Validation

The solver is a port of ShellForm's `formfind.py`. A script runs ShellForm itself on six cases (edge- and corner-supported, with and without lateral load, and the free-edge inset search) and stores the results as test fixtures. The browser solver matches their coordinates to 9.4 × 10⁻¹⁴ m and their reactions to 10⁻⁸, and every form is in equilibrium to a relative residual below 10⁻⁹. The tests run on every push.

## What this does not show

- It finds forms; it does not design shells. Thickness is not sized, and buckling, bending under non-funicular loads, reinforcement and supports are not checked.
- The stress shown is a bar force over its strip of shell, for orientation only.
- Carbon counts concrete only, so it is a lower bound.
