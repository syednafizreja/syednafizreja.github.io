I work on computational design and mechanics of structures: finding efficient forms and checking them with finite-element analysis and seismic design. Concrete shells are my current test case, and embodied carbon is one of the design variables. My code is open and tested.

<More label="See the thesis and the three records">

My bachelor thesis at Southwest Petroleum University in Chengdu was the full hand design of a four-storey reinforced concrete frame for a seismic site in Sichuan, to the Chinese codes. Three open records build on it. ShellBench follows thin-shell floors through the seismic design and embodied carbon of whole RC buildings, and is released with its dataset. ShellForm measures the carbon cost of making form-found concrete shells earthquake-resistant. A third study compares stress-line ribs with an optimised thickness on one shell, at the same concrete volume.

</More>

Before this I spent two and a half years as a project engineer in precision manufacturing, running quality control, durability testing and root-cause analysis. That work taught me to trust a result only after it has been checked against something independent, and it is why every project on this site states its benchmark and what it does not show.

Since 2024 I have also worked as an independent research collaborator with a PhD researcher at Hohai University, on BIM, nano-modified asphalt, durability modelling and life-cycle assessment.

I studied in China on a full Chinese Government Scholarship and have lived and worked there since 2019. Outside work I train in Muay Thai and MMA, and read on psychology and decision-making.
