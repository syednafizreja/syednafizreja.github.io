I work on computational mechanics of materials and structures: constitutive modelling, finite-element analysis, form-finding and seismic design. Concrete shells are one test case, self-centring NiTi systems another, and embodied carbon is the design variable that joins them. Released code is open and tested.

<More label="See the thesis and the studies">

My bachelor thesis at Southwest Petroleum University in Chengdu was the full hand design of a four-storey reinforced concrete frame for a seismic site in Sichuan, to the Chinese codes. The studies below start from that frame.

ShellBench carries a shell floor through the seismic design and the A1–A3 carbon of a whole reinforced-concrete building. In 32 of 33 buildings the shell floor lowers that carbon, by a median of 5.7%.

ShellForm asks whether putting the earthquake into the form-finding saves carbon. In 79 of 84 paired cells the two strategies differ by less than 5%. Thickness is set by buckling or by a minimum, and the difference disappears under the robustness checks.

The ribs study holds the concrete volume fixed on one 12 × 12 m shell. An orthogonal grid gains 16.05%. An optimised thickness gains 25.74%. Stress-line ribs fall below the plain shell. With an imperfection the thickness lead is 15.03%.

A floor study, not yet released, has one 3 m vault bay and no carbon optimum. At a rise of 0.60 m the smooth shell keeps 0.40763 of the flat extensional stiffness, and one rib cross keeps 0.38984. Those cells do not share one diaphragm ratio.

Between the thesis and these studies I spent two and a half years as a project and quality-control engineer in precision medical-device manufacturing, working on nickel-titanium (NiTi) alloy components: micro-CNC machining, durability and fatigue testing, and root-cause failure analysis. That is where I learned shape-memory and superelastic alloys at first hand, and it is why the diagrid study models a NiTi self-centring fuse as a constitutive problem rather than treating it as an exotic material. It also taught me to trust a result only after it has been checked against something independent, which is why every project on this site states its benchmark and what it does not show.

Since 2024 I have also worked as an independent research collaborator with a PhD researcher at Hohai University, on BIM, nano-modified asphalt, durability modelling and life-cycle assessment.

I studied in China on a full Chinese Government Scholarship and have lived and worked there since 2019. Outside work I train in Muay Thai and MMA, and read on psychology and decision-making.
