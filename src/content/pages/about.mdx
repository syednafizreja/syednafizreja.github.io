I want the work to stay close to the mechanics: a model that can be rerun, a figure that can be argued with, and the limit written next to the result.

The record is short. A BEng in Civil Engineering at Southwest Petroleum University, on a Chinese Government Scholarship, with a hand-designed four-storey seismic frame as the thesis. Then two years of cyclic-durability testing on superelastic NiTi in precision manufacturing, including micro-CNC metrology and failure analysis. The employer is not named here.

What I like is the disagreement between a drawing and a check. Stress-line ribs can look right and still lose buckling capacity. A superelastic fuse can cut residual drift and still raise peak drift and carbon. I would rather keep that result than smooth it.

The open questions are 3D thermomechanical shape-memory models, imperfection-sensitive shells and enriched continua, and lightweight systems where seismic performance and embodied carbon are set together.
